Community Health Nursing (NUR3203) - Core Notes

Lecture 1: Introduction to Community Health

Public Health is the science and art focusing on preventing disease and promoting individual health through collective interventions. Health Equity aims to achieve equality in access to healthcare and services across all individuals. A healthy environment involves clean air, safe water, and good housing.

Lecture 2: Determinants of Health

Health determinants include Biological Factors (genetics), Socio-cultural conditions (lifestyle choices), Environmental conditions, and Socioeconomic status (like GDP per capita). Education, particularly for women, is one of the most critical factors influencing health outcomes, as low education links to poor health and higher stress.

Lecture 3: Principles and Types of Diseases

Diseases manifest at various levels of biological organization (Cell → Tissue → Organ → System). Types include: Infectious (e.g., Influenza, Malaria), Non-infectious (e.g., Diabetes, Heart disease), Genetic (e.g., Sickle Cell Anemia), Chronic (e.g., Asthma, Hypertension), and Acute (e.g., Appendicitis, common cold).

Lecture 4: Prevalent Diseases in Saudi Society

Chronic diseases heavily impact the Saudi population. Diabetes affects approximately 19% of adults, with 44% at risk. Hypertension affects 3.4% of adults over 30. Obesity, a major lifestyle-related disease, has a prevalence of 20.2%. Dengue fever is a notable infectious disease transmitted by the Aedes aegypti mosquito.

Lecture 5: Immunizations & Screening

Vaccines stimulate the immune system to produce long-term immunity. Herd immunity protects the unvaccinated when a sufficient percentage of the population is immunized. The Saudi National Immunization Schedule includes childhood vaccines, Hajj/Umrah vaccines (like Meningococcal and Influenza), and vaccines for the elderly/chronic disease patients.

Lecture 6: Lifestyle & Cultural Habits

Healthy lifestyles require balanced nutrition, physical activity, and healthy sleep patterns. Negative cultural habits, such as consanguineous marriage (marrying close relatives), significantly increase the probability of transmitting genetic diseases like Thalassemia and Sickle Cell Anemia.
